# Options Made Easy

By Guy Cohen

Guy Cohen's book is designed to simplify the complex world of options for the individual investor. It uses a visual approach to help traders understand how different strategies work and when to use them.

## The Visual Approach: Risk Profiles
The book's greatest strength is its use of "Risk/Reward Profiles"—graphs that clearly show:
- **Maximum Profit:** The most you can make.
- **Maximum Risk:** The most you can lose.
- **Breakeven Point:** Where the trade starts making money.

## Core Concepts for Beginners

### 1. The Four Basic Trades
- **Buying a Call:** Bullish speculation with limited risk.
- **Buying a Put:** Bearish speculation with limited risk.
- **Selling a Call (Naked):** Extremely high risk; generally discouraged for beginners.
- **Selling a Put:** Bullish/Neutral strategy; used to acquire stock at a lower price or generate income.

### 2. Time Decay (Theta) and Leverage
Cohen explains these concepts in plain English:
- **Leverage:** Controlling a large amount of stock with a small amount of money.
- **Time Decay:** Why options lose value as they approach expiration.

### 3. "The Strategy Table"
The book provides a handy reference table to help traders pick a strategy based on their market outlook:
- **Bullish:** Long Calls, Bull Call Spreads, Naked Puts (for some).
- **Bearish:** Long Puts, Bear Put Spreads.
- **Neutral:** Covered Calls, Credit Spreads, Iron Condors.
- **Volatile:** Straddles and Strangles.

## Popular Strategies Detailed

### 1. Covered Calls
The "bread and butter" strategy for most conservative investors. Cohen explains how to pick the right strike and expiration to maximize income while protecting against a crash.

### 2. Spreads: Reducing Cost and Risk
- **Vertical Spreads:** Buying one option and selling another of the same type but a different strike.
- **Visualizing Spreads:** How selling an option "finances" the purchase of another, lowering the total cost of the trade.

### 3. The Iron Condor
A popular neutral strategy that combines a Bull Put Spread and a Bear Call Spread. Cohen shows how to set this up for "consistent income" in a range-bound market.

## Conclusion
*Options Made Easy* is the perfect starting point for someone who feels overwhelmed by the math of Natenberg or McMillan. It focuses on the *application* of strategies and provides a clear, visual framework for decision-making.
