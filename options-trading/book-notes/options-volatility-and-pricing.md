# Option Volatility and Pricing

By Sheldon Natenberg

This is the definitive guide to understanding how options are priced and how volatility affects those prices. It is a cornerstone text for professional traders, market makers, and institutional investors.

## Key Theoretical Foundations

### 1. The Nature of Risk
Natenberg defines risk not just as "losing money," but as the uncertainty of future outcomes. Options are the primary tool for quantifying and trading this uncertainty.

### 2. The Black-Scholes Framework
The book provides a rigorous walkthrough of the Black-Scholes model, explaining the five key inputs:
- **Underlying Price**
- **Exercise (Strike) Price**
- **Time to Expiration**
- **Interest Rates**
- **Volatility** (The only unknown and the most important variable).

### 3. The "Greeks" in Detail
Natenberg provides the most thorough explanation of the Greeks available in literature:
- **Delta:** The "Hedge Ratio"—how many shares of stock you need to offset the option's risk.
- **Gamma:** The "Curvature"—how the Delta changes as the stock moves.
- **Theta:** The "Rental Fee"—the daily cost of owning the option.
- **Vega:** The "Volatility Risk"—how much you stand to gain or lose from a 1% change in IV.

## Advanced Volatility Concepts

### 1. The Volatility Surface
Understanding that volatility is not a single number, but a "surface" that varies across strike prices (Skew) and time (Term Structure).
- **Smiles and Skews:** Why out-of-the-money puts often trade at higher IVs than out-of-the-money calls (fear vs. greed).

### 2. Volatility Arbitrage
The core strategy of many hedge funds—identifying when the market's implied volatility is significantly different from what the trader believes the actual future volatility will be.

## Practical Trading Applications
- **Spreads and Combinations:** How to build positions that isolate specific Greeks (e.g., a "Vega positive" but "Delta neutral" trade).
- **The Market Maker's Edge:** How professionals manage large portfolios of options by focusing on their net Greek exposure rather than individual trades.

## Conclusion
If you want to understand the *why* behind option prices, this is the book. It transforms options from simple directional bets into a sophisticated language for trading risk and volatility.
