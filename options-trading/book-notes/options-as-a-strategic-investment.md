# Options as a Strategic Investment

By Lawrence G. McMillan

Often referred to as the "Bible of Options Trading," this comprehensive guide covers almost every conceivable options strategy and its application in various market conditions.

## Key Themes
- **Risk Control:** Using options to hedge existing positions.
- **Income Generation:** Using options to enhance returns on a portfolio.
- **Speculation:** Using leverage to profit from market movements with defined risk.

## Core Strategies Covered

### 1. Covered Call Writing
- Selling call options against stock you already own.
- **Goal:** Generate income and provide a small buffer against a decline in the stock price.
- **Trade-off:** You limit your upside potential if the stock price rises significantly.

### 2. Buying Calls and Puts
- Buying calls for bullish bets and puts for bearish bets.
- **Goal:** Leverage and limited risk (you can only lose the premium paid).
- **Challenge:** Time decay (theta) works against the buyer.

### 3. Neutral Strategies
- **Straddles and Strangles:** Buying both a call and a put. Used when you expect a big move but don't know the direction.
- **Butterfly Spreads:** Selling two options at a middle strike and buying one above and one below. Used when you expect the stock to stay within a tight range.

### 4. Spread Strategies
- **Bull Spreads and Bear Spreads:** Using vertical spreads to reduce the cost of a position and define both maximum risk and maximum reward.
- **Calendar Spreads (Time Spreads):** Selling a short-term option and buying a long-term option of the same strike. Profitable if the stock remains stable.

### 5. Mathematical and Statistical Analysis
- McMillan emphasizes the importance of understanding **Expected Value** and **Probability**.
- Use of the Black-Scholes model and other pricing models to identify mispriced options.

### 6. Volatility Trading
- Understanding the difference between **Historical Volatility** and **Implied Volatility**.
- Strategies for trading "Volatility Skew" and "Term Structure."

### 7. Index Options and Futures
- Using broad market indices to hedge whole portfolios or speculate on macro trends.

## Strategic Principles
- **Selection:** Choosing the right strategy for the specific market outlook.
- **Follow-up Action:** Managing the position after it's been opened (rolling, closing, or adjusting).
- **Discipline:** Adhering to a plan and managing risk consistently.

## Conclusion
McMillan’s book is an essential resource for any serious options trader. It moves beyond simple "betting" and teaches how to use options as professional-grade tools for portfolio management and strategic trading.
