# Option Volatility and Pricing Strategies

By Sheldon Natenberg

This book is widely considered one of the most important texts on the theory of option pricing and volatility. It is essential for traders who want to understand the "Greeks" and how professional market makers approach the market.

## Core Concepts

### 1. Probability and the Normal Distribution
Natenberg starts with the basics of probability, explaining how option prices are essentially a reflection of the market's expectation of the probability of future price movements.

### 2. Option Pricing Models
- **Black-Scholes Model:** The foundation of modern option pricing.
- **Binomial Model:** A more intuitive, step-by-step approach to pricing.
- Natenberg emphasizes that models are just tools—they provide a "theoretical value," but the market price is determined by supply and demand.

### 3. The Greeks: Measuring Risk
The "Greeks" are the most critical part of the book for active traders:
- **Delta:** Sensitivity to the underlying asset's price change.
- **Gamma:** Sensitivity of Delta to the underlying asset's price change.
- **Theta:** Sensitivity to the passage of time (time decay).
- **Vega:** Sensitivity to changes in implied volatility.
- **Rho:** Sensitivity to changes in interest rates.

### 4. Understanding Volatility
This is the heart of the book.
- **Historical Volatility:** How much the stock has moved in the past.
- **Implied Volatility (IV):** What the market expects the stock to move in the future, as "implied" by the current option price.
- **Volatility Skew:** The fact that different strikes often have different implied volatilities.
- **Term Structure:** How implied volatility differs across different expiration dates.

### 5. Strategies and Risk Management
Natenberg doesn't just list strategies; he explains them through the lens of the Greeks.
- **Delta Neutral Trading:** Strategies like straddles and strangles where you are betting on volatility rather than price direction.
- **Spreads:** Using combinations of options to offset certain Greeks (e.g., a vertical spread reduces Vega and Theta risk compared to buying a single option).

## Professional Perspectives
- **The Role of the Market Maker:** How professionals manage their "book" of options to remain delta neutral and profit from the "bid-ask spread" and "volatility edge."
- **Arbitrage:** Identifying and profiting from pricing discrepancies.

## Conclusion
*Option Volatility and Pricing* is not a "get rich quick" book. It is a deep, mathematical, and conceptual dive into the mechanics of options. Mastery of Natenberg's principles is what separates professional option traders from retail speculators.
