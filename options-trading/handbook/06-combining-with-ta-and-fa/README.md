# Section 6 — Combining with Technical and Fundamental Analysis

Options are not a standalone discipline. The most effective options traders combine options mechanics and volatility analysis with directional insight from technical analysis (TA) and stock selection quality from fundamental analysis (FA). Each discipline answers a different question:

| Discipline | Primary question answered |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Options mechanics / Greeks** | How to structure the trade and manage risk |
| **Volatility analysis** | When options are cheap or expensive; what IV environment you're in |
| **Technical analysis** | When to enter, where the stock is going near-term, what levels matter |
| **Fundamental analysis** | Which stocks to trade, the quality of the underlying, whether to own or avoid |

## Why Integration Matters

- An iron condor built on a technically broken stock about to test all-time lows is a bad trade regardless of the options structure.
- A LEAPS call on a deeply undervalued company works far better than on an overvalued stock.
- A technically strong setup with a clear catalyst and cheap options is the highest-conviction combination available.

Without TA and FA context, options positions lack directional edge and are reduced to pure volatility bets — which have a place, but represent a narrower set of opportunities.
