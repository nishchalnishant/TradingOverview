# Section 3 — Volatility

Volatility is the central variable in options trading. Understanding the difference between historical and implied volatility, how volatility is distributed across strikes and expirations, and when volatility is cheap or expensive is what separates skilled options traders from directional gamblers.

## Key Concepts

- **Historical volatility (HV):** What the stock actually did. Backward-looking.
- **Implied volatility (IV):** What the options market predicts the stock will do. Forward-looking.
- **The vol premium:** On average, IV > HV. Options tend to be overpriced. This is the structural edge for option sellers.
- **Skew:** Puts on equities almost always trade at higher IV than calls. This reflects demand for downside protection.
- **Term structure:** IV varies across expiration dates. Near-term IV usually rises around events; longer-dated IV is more stable.

---

## Why Volatility Matters More Than Direction

A directional trader asks: "Which way will the stock move?"
A volatility trader asks: "How much will it move, and are options priced cheaply or expensively relative to that expected move?"

Even if you are right about direction, buying overpriced options (high IV) often results in losses when IV reverts to normal. The best options trade combines correct directional view with cheap or fair volatility.
