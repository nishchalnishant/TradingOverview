# Technical Analysis Using Multiple Timeframes

By Brian Shannon

## Core Philosophy: "Only Price Pays"
The central theme of the book is that price action is the ultimate indicator. Technical analysis is used to identify the trend, manage risk, and find low-risk/high-reward entry points.

## The Importance of Multiple Timeframes
Using multiple timeframes allows a trader to see the "big picture" (the trend) while finding precise entry points on shorter timeframes.
- **Long-term timeframe (Daily/Weekly):** Used to identify the primary trend and major support/resistance levels.
- **Intermediate-term timeframe (Hourly/30-min):** Used to look for patterns and setups within the primary trend.
- **Short-term timeframe (5-min/1-min):** Used for timing entries and exits.

## Key Concepts

### 1. The Four Stages of the Market Cycle
Understanding where a stock is in its cycle is crucial:
- **Stage 1: Accumulation:** Sideways action after a downtrend. Institutional buying.
- **Stage 2: Markup:** The uptrend. Higher highs and higher lows. This is where most profits are made.
- **Stage 3: Distribution:** Sideways action after an uptrend. Institutional selling.
- **Stage 4: Markdown:** The downtrend. Lower highs and lower lows.

### 2. Support and Resistance
- Support is where demand exceeds supply.
- Resistance is where supply exceeds demand.
- **Role Reversal:** Once support is broken, it becomes resistance. Once resistance is broken, it becomes support.

### 3. Moving Averages
Brian Shannon heavily uses the **Volume Weighted Average Price (VWAP)** and simple moving averages (10, 20, 50, and 200-period).
- Moving averages help identify the trend and provide dynamic support/resistance.
- The slope of the moving average indicates the strength of the trend.

### 4. Anchored VWAP (AVWAP)
A key tool introduced/popularized by Shannon. It allows you to anchor the VWAP calculation to a specific significant event (like an earnings report, a gap, or a major high/low) to see the average price participants have paid since 그 event.

## Trading Strategy: Alignment of Trends
The highest probability trades occur when multiple timeframes are in alignment.
- **Uptrend:** Buy when the short-term timeframe pulls back to support on the intermediate timeframe, provided the long-term trend is up.
- **Downtrend:** Sell/Short when the short-term timeframe bounces to resistance on the intermediate timeframe, provided the long-term trend is down.

## Risk Management
- **Stop Losses:** Always use them. Place them just below support in an uptrend.
- **Risk/Reward Ratio:** Aim for at least 3:1.
- **Position Sizing:** Never risk more than a small percentage of your total capital on a single trade.

## Conclusion
Technical Analysis Using Multiple Timeframes provides a structured approach to the markets by combining price action, volume, and time. By understanding the market cycle and using multiple timeframes to confirm trends, traders can significantly improve their odds of success.
