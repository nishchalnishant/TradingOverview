# Intermarket Analysis

## Concept

**Intermarket analysis** examines the relationships between different asset classes — equities, bonds, commodities, and currencies — to find confirmation or divergence signals that a single chart cannot reveal. Markets do not exist in isolation; capital flows between them, and the relationships that emerge provide a macro framework for interpreting technical signals.

The core idea: **before trading a signal in isolation, check whether other related markets are confirming or contradicting it**.

---

## The Four Major Asset Classes

| Asset Class | Primary Drivers | Common Instruments |
|---|---|---|
| Equities | Economic growth, earnings, risk appetite | S&P 500, NASDAQ, sector ETFs |
| Bonds (Fixed Income) | Interest rate expectations, inflation, credit risk | US 10Y Treasury, TLT, HYG |
| Commodities | Supply/demand, inflation, USD strength | Gold, Oil (WTI/Brent), Copper, Wheat |
| Currencies (FX) | Interest rate differentials, trade flows, risk sentiment | DXY, EUR/USD, USD/JPY, AUD/USD |

## Bond Yields vs Equity Valuations

### The Inverse Relationship

Rising bond yields mean rising borrowing costs and a higher discount rate for future cash flows. This generally **pressures equity valuations**, particularly for growth stocks where most of the value lies in future earnings.

```
Yield rises  →  discount rate rises  →  present value of future earnings falls  →  equity P/E contracts
Yield falls  →  discount rate falls  →  present value of future earnings rises  →  equity P/E expands
```

### Practical Application

| Scenario | Bond Market Signal | Equity Implication |
|---|---|---|
| 10Y yield breaks to multi-year high | Bond market pricing in higher rates for longer | Headwind for high-P/E growth stocks; banks may benefit |
| 10Y yield falling sharply | Growth fears or flight to safety | Risk-off; equities may fall even as yields fall (recession fear) |
| Yield curve flattening (2Y approaching 10Y) | Fed tightening faster than long-end | Caution signal; historically precedes slowdowns |
| Yield curve inverts (2Y > 10Y) | Recession indicator (see Leading Indicators below) | Historically negative for equities 6–18 months out |

**TA Application:** If a stock or equity index is forming a bullish pattern but the 10Y yield is spiking, the macro headwind may invalidate the technical setup or reduce its probability of success.

## USD Strength vs Commodities

### The Inverse Relationship

Most major commodities are priced in US dollars. When the dollar strengthens, it takes fewer dollars to buy the same commodity — all else equal, commodity prices in USD fall. When the dollar weakens, commodity prices rise in USD terms.

```
DXY up  →  commodities priced in USD become more expensive for non-USD buyers  →  demand falls  →  price falls (in USD)
DXY down  →  commodities cheaper in USD  →  demand rises  →  price rises (in USD)
```

### Correlation Table

| Commodity | USD Correlation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Gold | Strong negative | Gold is also driven by real interest rates (real yield = nominal yield − inflation) |
| Oil (WTI/Brent) | Moderate negative | Supply/demand and geopolitics dominate at times; not always tightly correlated |
| Copper | Moderate negative | Also heavily driven by China demand (see Leading Indicators) |
| Agricultural (wheat, corn) | Weak to moderate negative | Local supply/demand often dominates |

**TA Application:** If gold is forming a bullish breakout pattern, check the DXY. If DXY is also breaking out to the upside, the gold pattern is working against a macro headwind. Wait for DXY confirmation.

## Commodity-Linked Currencies

Some currencies are deeply tied to commodity exports. Their exchange rates tend to move with commodity prices.

| Currency | Primary Commodity Link | Key Driver |
|---|---|---|
| AUD (Australian Dollar) | Iron ore, coal, gold | Chinese demand for commodities; RBA rate differentials |
| CAD (Canadian Dollar) | Oil (crude) | WTI/Brent price; US-Canada trade relationship |
| NOK (Norwegian Krone) | North Sea oil | Brent crude; European energy market |
| BRL (Brazilian Real) | Iron ore, soybeans, oil | China demand; global risk appetite |
| CLP (Chilean Peso) | Copper | Copper price; one of the tightest commodity-currency correlations |
| RUB (Russian Ruble) | Oil and natural gas | Brent crude price; heavily influenced by geopolitics and sanctions |

### How to Use This

- **AUD/USD as a risk barometer:** AUD tends to rise in risk-on environments (higher commodity demand, higher interest rate differentials vs USD) and fall in risk-off (flight to USD safety, lower commodity demand).
- **CAD/JPY as a risk sentiment proxy:** CAD is commodity-linked (risk-on); JPY is a safe haven (risk-off). CAD/JPY rising = risk-on environment; falling = risk-off. Useful for gauging market sentiment.
- **Divergence signals:** If oil is rising but CAD is not following, something may be counteracting the commodity link (e.g. broader USD strength, Canadian economic concerns). This divergence is worth investigating.

## Leading Indicators from Intermarket Analysis

These intermarket signals tend to lead other asset class moves, making them useful for positioning before a move is fully priced in.

### 1. Yield Curve Inversion (2Y–10Y Spread)

When the 2-year Treasury yield exceeds the 10-year yield, the yield curve is **inverted**. This is one of the most historically reliable recession predictors.

| Spread | Signal |
|---|---|
| 2Y–10Y > 0 (normal) | Normal economic conditions; banks earn positive carry |
| 2Y–10Y approaching 0 (flattening) | Late cycle; rate hikes are catching up to long-term growth expectations |
| 2Y–10Y < 0 (inverted) | Recession signal; historically recession follows within 6–24 months |
| Re-steepening from deep inversion | Often the most dangerous moment — recession arrival is imminent |

**TA Use:** An inverted yield curve does not mean sell everything immediately. Markets often continue higher for months after inversion. But it raises the threshold for adding long equity exposure and flags that the risk/reward is shifting.

### 2. Copper Price as an Economic Barometer

Copper is used in construction, manufacturing, and electronics. Its price is a real-time vote on global economic health, earning it the nickname "Dr. Copper."

- **Copper rising:** Manufacturing expansion expected; global growth outlook improving.
- **Copper falling:** Demand slowing; manufacturing contraction; China growth concerns.
- **Divergence:** Equities making new highs while copper falls is a bearish divergence — the equity market may not be accurately pricing the growth slowdown.

### 3. Credit Spreads

**High-yield (HY) credit spreads** measure the additional yield investors demand to hold corporate junk bonds vs US Treasuries. Wider spreads = more fear of corporate defaults = risk-off.

| Credit Spread | Market Signal |
|---|---|
| Spreads tightening | Risk appetite high; corporate health improving; equities tend to rise |
| Spreads widening | Risk aversion rising; often precedes equity corrections |
| Spreads very wide and starting to narrow | Often a contrarian buy signal for equities |
| Spreads at historical extremes | Systemic stress; potential for forced selling across asset classes |

**TA Use:** If equities are at support and forming a bullish pattern, but HY credit spreads are widening sharply, the technical pattern has a lower probability of success. Converging signals (technical + credit) increase confidence.

### 4. Gold vs Copper Ratio

The Gold/Copper ratio compares two metals with very different demand profiles: gold (safe haven, no industrial use) vs copper (industrial demand proxy).

- **Ratio rising:** More demand for safety relative to industrial activity — risk-off, potential equity weakness ahead.
- **Ratio falling:** Industrial demand outpacing safe-haven demand — risk-on, equities likely improving.

## How TA Traders Use Intermarket Signals

### Confirmation

A technical signal is stronger when intermarket context aligns:

| Technical Signal | Confirming Intermarket Context |
|---|---|
| S&P 500 bullish breakout | HY credit spreads tightening; DXY stable or falling; copper rising |
| Gold breakout | DXY falling or rolling over; real yields falling; geopolitical risk rising |
| Oil bullish reversal | DXY stable; global growth data improving; AUD/USD and CAD stable or rising |
| USD/JPY rising (risk-on USD) | Equities rising; credit spreads tightening; VIX falling |

### Divergence Warning

A technical pattern is weaker when intermarket signals contradict it:

| Technical Signal | Contradicting Intermarket Signal | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Equity index at support, bullish candle | HY spreads widening; copper falling | Low probability bounce; consider smaller size or skip |
| Gold near resistance after rally | DXY breaking out to new highs | Resistance likely to hold; technical short setup more viable |
| Oil breaking out | DXY also breaking out | USD headwind may cap the oil rally; reduce target |

### Intermarket Checklist Before a Trade

1. What is the DXY doing? (relevant for commodities and USD-linked assets)
2. What are 10Y yields doing? (relevant for equities, especially growth)
3. Are credit spreads tightening or widening? (risk-on or risk-off confirmation)
4. Is copper confirming or diverging from equities?
5. Is the commodity-linked currency confirming a commodity trade?

## Practical Examples

**Example 1: Long Gold Setup**
- Technical: Gold at key support with bullish engulfing candle.
- Intermarket check: DXY at resistance and turning down; 10Y real yields falling; VIX elevated.
- Verdict: All intermarket signals confirm the gold long. Higher confidence.

**Example 2: Long Equity Index Setup**
- Technical: S&P 500 at 200MA support with strong volume.
- Intermarket check: HY credit spreads spiking; copper breaking multi-month support; yield curve deeply inverted.
- Verdict: Intermarket signals warn against aggressive long. Smaller size or wait for intermarket confirmation before adding.

**Example 3: Long AUD/USD Setup**
- Technical: AUD/USD at key support level, RSI oversold.
- Intermarket check: Iron ore prices stabilizing; DXY at resistance; Chinese PMI improving.
- Verdict: Commodity-currency link supports the technical setup. Intermarket confirms.
