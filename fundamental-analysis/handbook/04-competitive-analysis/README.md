# Section 04 — Competitive Analysis

Numbers describe what a business has done. Competitive analysis explains why it has been able to do it — and whether it can continue. Understanding a company's competitive position is what separates a transient earnings windfall from a sustainable compounding machine.

---

## Why Competitive Analysis Matters for Valuation

A DCF model assigns terminal value based on an assumed perpetual growth rate and terminal margin. The legitimacy of those assumptions depends entirely on competitive position:

- A company with a durable moat can sustain high margins and ROIC for decades — high terminal value is justified.
- A company without a moat will see margins compete away over time — a high terminal margin assumption is unjustified.

The quality of the business determines the multiple it deserves in relative valuation and the terminal value in DCF. Competitive analysis is how you assess that quality.

## The Quality Hierarchy

When assessing investment quality, think in layers:

```
Industry Attractiveness (macro competitive forces)
        ↓
Company Competitive Position (moat within the industry)
        ↓
Management Quality (execution and capital allocation)
        ↓
Financial Quality (earnings quality, cash conversion, balance sheet)
```

An exceptional company in a terrible industry faces structural headwinds that even great management can't fully overcome. Ideally, all four layers are favorable.
