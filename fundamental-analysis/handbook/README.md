## Core Philosophy

Fundamental analysis rests on one premise: **the intrinsic value of a business can be estimated, and when the market price diverges significantly from that value, an opportunity exists.**

Three questions drive every FA process:

1. **What is the business worth?** (Valuation)
2. **How durable are its earnings and competitive position?** (Quality analysis)
3. **What is the market pricing in, and why might it be wrong?** (Gap analysis)

---

## Key Principles

**Earnings vs. Cash Flow:** Always reconcile reported earnings with cash flow from operations. Earnings are an opinion; cash is a fact.

**Business quality before price:** A mediocre business at a cheap price is still a mediocre business. Understand the moat before modeling the numbers.

**Margin of safety:** Never buy at fair value — build in a cushion. The future is uncertain, and your model will be wrong.

**Context matters:** Ratios and multiples are only meaningful relative to history, peers, and the macro environment. No number is cheap or expensive in isolation.

**Management is underrated:** Capital allocation decisions compound over years. A great business with poor capital allocators can destroy value; a decent business with excellent operators can create it.

```
Financial Statements → Financial Ratios → Valuation Methods
        ↓                                        ↓
Earnings Quality ←——— Investment Thesis ←——— Competitive Analysis
```
