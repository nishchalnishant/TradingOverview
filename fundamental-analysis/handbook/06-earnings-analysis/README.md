# Section 06 — Earnings Analysis

Earnings reports are the primary recurring event that moves stock prices in the short to medium term. This section covers two distinct but related skills:

1. **Earnings Quality Analysis** — assessing whether reported earnings accurately reflect the economic reality of the business, or whether they are distorted by accounting choices, manipulation, or non-cash items.

2. **Earnings Season Playbook** — understanding how to position around earnings announcements, read management communications, and exploit post-earnings price dynamics.

---

## Why Earnings Analysis Matters

Earnings are the numerator in P/E and the basis for most valuation multiples. If reported earnings are inflated by aggressive accounting, every multiple based on those earnings is misleading. An investor who pays 15× artificially high earnings may actually be paying 25× normalized earnings.

Conversely, earnings that are conservatively reported (e.g., heavy D&A, significant SBC expense, legitimate restructuring charges) may understate true earnings power. Identifying companies with conservatively reported but high-quality earnings is a source of alpha.

## The Core Principle

**Earnings are an estimate; cash flow is a fact.**

Financial accounting allows significant discretion in timing revenue and expense recognition. A sophisticated analyst never takes reported earnings at face value. The first question on any income statement: does this match what the cash flow statement shows?

When the two diverge persistently, something is wrong. Either:
- The business is deteriorating (real economic problem)
- Accounting is masking reality (potential manipulation)
- Non-cash charges are temporarily depressing earnings (potential opportunity)

The direction of the divergence tells you which scenario you're in.
